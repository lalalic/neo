# Creative Strategies — Licensed Short-Drama CPS

**Purpose:** A small library of repeatable editorial hypotheses. This document owns *creative strategy*; the actual selected shots, edits, overlays, sound, CTA and revision belong to each run's Markcut `video.md`. No separate rendering system or generic AI prompt may override that file.

## Evidence and limits

- Creator-platform research: Tencent Ads' short-drama guidance describes **commentary + hook + closing suspense**, and emotion/reversal/identity-contrast/fuel-edit structures: https://www.teng-ad.com/jd/3892.html . These are editorial heuristics, **not measured results for this project**.
- Xiaohongshu audience research: coverage of XHS drama memes describes **the viewer-as-commentator**, everyday talk and questioning absurd drama behavior: https://www.bianews.com/news/details?id=182099 . A 2026 marketing case argues for everyday-language, authentic reactions instead of generic official pitches: https://www.topsky.com/product/xiaohongshu/684 . These are secondary sources, not original individual XHS notes.
- **Original XHS note follow-up (2026-11-02):** A signed-in Worknode browser exposed the search/favorites index; three public note detail pages and visible comments were actually inspected, as listed below. Afterward XHS displayed **DEVICE_LIMIT / re-login required** on further searches. Do not imply all saved notes, all comments, or creator videos were inspected; do not infer audience retention or CPS conversion from note likes.
- Cautionary industry coverage: editors who overcompress stories or copy near-identical slices risk weak coherence and duplicate content: https://www.bianews.com/news/details?id=178185 . Keep one intelligible emotional arc, not random visual highlights.
- **RS Boost license overrides all creative inspiration.** Agreement §III: https://cps.reelshort.com/user/agreement . You may crop, re-order and compile *authorized Advertising Materials*, but must not mislead or distort, strip marks, use materials disconnected from links, or **re-dub/narrate/spoil without prior consent** (§III.3). A convincing real-user POV is not permission to add narration. Use unaltered original audio for production-bound versions; keep POV voice scripts as **internal experiments only** pending explicit provider consent. Avoid irrelevant text/audio as well.
- External platform policies also apply; titles must match actual content. XHS's published rules reject bait-and-switch headlines and misleading interactions: https://school.xiaohongshu.com/rule/detail/10127 . This guide does not imply XHS is an approved RS Boost destination.

**Lesson:** XHS technique posts advertise *how easily a clip can be made*, whereas our objective is whether a real viewer is emotionally motivated to watch the next part. Do not select a template solely because its production is automated. The direct note corpus is small and has no verified view-through or affiliate conversion metrics.

Use three separate lenses: (1) **source drama truth** — actual quotes, actions and relationships; (2) **viewer motivation** — embarrassment, agency, rejection, anticipation; (3) **conversion destination** — clear series title and legitimate promotion link. Treat the commenter's question *“what happens next?”* as a hypothesis to test, not a fact about this campaign. Notes about public monetization are business context, not creative performance data.

## Workflow: source video → specific beats → ranked hooks

1. **Watch the entire licensed source** (not only a contact sheet); log time ranges in source seconds and copy the actual line/expression/action, with before/after context. Each hook must map to concrete evidence, not to an invented twist.
2. Collect **5–10 candidate beats**. Rate each on *immediate comprehensibility*, *human emotion*, *visual/speech clarity*, *unfinished question*, and *suitability to channel/legal scope* (0–2 each). A dramatic but untrue headline scores zero on suitability, regardless of sum.
3. Choose three materially different *hypotheses*, not just three different sentences atop the same 18 seconds. Keep consistent scene geography and whose point of view is speaking. First frame should show the specific emotional conflict, not a logo, greeting or slow exposition.
4. Put the most informative image/line inside the **first ~1–2 seconds** (starting hypothesis, not proven magic number). Let the original scene establish context for ~5–12 seconds, then resolve one small issue **while leaving an authentic unanswered choice**. Add a short end cue, with the promotion URL attached at the *platform-approved location*; confirm real redirect to the particular title before posting.
5. Make a `video.md` per variant using installed Markcut. Avoid long static CTA slates: keep the cut aligned to the story; place legible text outside faces/subtitles/copyright marks. Preserve licensed soundtrack and dialogue unless individually approved. Verify/render **and watch with audio**.
6. Collect exact owner approval for the specific rendered revision **and separate posting authorization**. After allowed publication, use account-level WebHelm monitoring plus real CPS data; inspect retention and outbound click quality, not raw views alone.

## Reusable editorial templates

| Template | First beat (0–2s) | Story span | Ending | Default rights lane | Distinct hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Confrontation / Quote-first** | Actual strongest spoken line or reaction; caption quoting it faithfully | Rewind or continue to explain **who said it and why**, without false chronology | Stop at original unfinished decision, short honest watch cue | Original footage/dialogue, restrained factual text | Does an authentic argument outperform scene-setting? |
| **B. Empathy / Rejection-to-boundary** | Character's unmistakable rejection or embarrassment, not a fabricated victim story | Show others' expectations, then their actual self-respecting response | One emotional closure and an authentic unresolved relationship question | Original audio, no melodramatic replacement dubbing | Does relatability outperform confrontation? |
| **C. Viewer-as-commentator (text POV)** | Short, *accurate*, conversational on-screen observation like “They were waiting for a meltdown.” | Original clips that prove the observation; let characters speak | A genuine question supported by the scene, without pretending to be the character | **Only factual, relevant on-screen editorial text**; no synthetic VO | Does a human perspective make the premise easier to grasp? |
| **D. Personal voice reaction (permission-gated)** | Original observer's first-person reaction; distinguish their opinion from the characters | Let a short original shot breathe, with quoted reaction in a distinct voice | Human follow-up question, no invented dialogue or spoiler | **HOLD** until written provider authorization covering commentary/dubbing and the specific edited creative, plus platform compliance | Whether an identifiable real voice beats pure clip edits |

A/B/C must be **different editorial structures**, not a fake “A/B test” of caption fonts. D is a script-only research lane until the rights gate is satisfied. Never present any of these as a guaranteed viral formula.

## Pre-release review and measurement

The reviewer should be able to answer: (a) Who is the observer? (b) What changed for the character? (c) Why watch the next scene? (d) Does each on-screen statement match footage and licensing? (e) Is the whole video understandable *without* reading a long caption?

For an authorized test, keep creative ID × revision/hash × account ID × post ID × link mapping. Collect after the same elapsed time per version: impressions/views, 2s/6s hold or average watch time if exposed, completion, saves/shares/comments, link clicks, attributed qualifying installs/purchases and eligible CPS. **Not exposed ≠ 0.** A 14-day pilot without sufficient traffic is exploratory, not a claim of statistically significant superiority.
